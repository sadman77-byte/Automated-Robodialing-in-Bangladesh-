# Cybercrime Threat & Incident Analysis: Silent Phone Calls, Automated Robodialing, and Follow-Up Fraud Risks

**Source Reference:** [Daily Amar Desh – Silent Phone Call Scam & Cyber Security Report](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=63EnaTMGeZo)  
**Published Date:** November 9, 2026  

---

## 1. Executive Summary
Silent phone calls—where an unknown number rings, has no voice on the other end for a few seconds, and disconnects automatically—can serve as a reconnaissance stage for broader cyber and financial scams [00:00:02], [00:00:11], [00:01:02]. Threat actors use automated computer-controlled dialers against phone number lists gathered from apps, online shopping sites, or other sources to identify active numbers and target them later with social engineering and financial fraud lures [00:00:30], [00:00:37], [00:01:09].

---

## 2. Classification of Cybercrime
Based on the mechanisms and follow-on threats described in the report, this activity relates to:
1. **Automated Telephony Reconnaissance / Spam & Robocalling:** Using automated dialers to place bulk calls and detect whether targeted phone numbers are active [00:00:30], [00:00:44], [00:00:50].
2. **Follow-Up Social Engineering & Financial Fraud (Pretexting / Scams):** Leveraging verified active numbers to conduct subsequent scams such as fake job offers, lottery lures, or impersonation of bKash and bank officials to extract money or sensitive credentials [00:01:09], [00:02:05].

---

## 3. Attack Workflow & Techniques Highlighted
* **Number List Aggregation:** Phone numbers are collected from various apps, online shopping sites, or other sources [00:00:37].
* **Automated / Robot Dialing:** Computer-controlled robot dialers call thousands of numbers simultaneously [00:00:44].
* **Silent Call / Active Number Identification:** Answering the call can indicate to the operators that the number is active/reachable, while silence may also occur when no call center operator is available on the other end [00:00:50], [00:00:55], [00:01:02].
* **Secondary Scam Targeting:** Once a number is flagged as active, it may later receive fraudulent job offers, lottery winning lures, or calls pretending to be from bKash or banks [00:01:09].
* **Context Note:** The report notes that not every silent call is a scam trap, advising caution rather than panic [00:01:18].

---

## 4. Potential Impact
* **Increased Exposure to Targeted Scams:** Identification of an active phone number increases the likelihood of receiving follow-up phishing/social engineering calls or lures [00:01:02], [00:01:09].
* **Financial & Credential Risk:** If victims fall for follow-up impersonation or requests for PINs, passwords, or OTPs, they face significant financial and data risks [00:02:05], [00:02:39].

---

## 5. Defensive Recommendations & Mitigations
* **Configure Phone Call-Blocking & Spam Settings:**
  * **iPhone:** Enable **Silence Unknown Callers** under **Settings > Phone** [00:01:32].
  * **Android:** Use **Caller ID & Spam** or **Block numbers** in the Phone app settings (exact menu names may vary by device) [00:01:38], [00:01:44].
* **Exercise Caution with Unknown & Foreign Numbers:** Avoid answering unnecessary calls from unknown numbers, disconnect silent calls, and never call back strange or suspicious foreign numbers [00:01:52], [00:01:56], [00:02:00].
* **Protect PINs, Passwords & OTPs:** Never share PINs, passwords, or OTPs with anyone claiming to represent a bank or mobile financial service; verify directly through the organization's official number if needed [00:02:05], [00:02:13].
* **Educate & Protect Elderly Family Members:** Raise awareness among older family members—who may be targeted through trust or fear—and enable spam detection and call-blocking settings on their phones [00:02:20], [00:02:30].
